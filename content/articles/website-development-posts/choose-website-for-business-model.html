To choose the right website for your business model, start with how your customers buy. B2B firms need credibility, case studies and lead capture. B2C brands need product discovery and checkout. Marketplaces need multi-vendor systems. SaaS companies need trials and subscription billing. Local services need location pages and booking. Then match the site's structure and budget to that model.

This guide maps each business model to the website it needs.

What does a B2B website need?

First, B2B buyers research for weeks and involve several people. So a B2B website must build trust over time. Case studies, detailed service pages and expert articles all help. In addition, every page should offer a next step, such as a consultation or a quote request.

Our guide to building a lead generation website covers this in detail.

What does a B2C website need?

B2C buyers decide faster and often on a phone. Therefore, a website for your business model in B2C must be quick, visual and easy to buy from. Strong search, clear prices, reviews and a short checkout matter most. See our ecommerce website development guide.

What does a marketplace website need?

By contrast, a marketplace serves two groups: buyers and sellers. As a result, it needs seller sign-up, listing tools, payments that split between parties, ratings and dispute handling. That makes it closer to a software product than a normal website. In most cases, it needs custom web development.

What does a SaaS website need?

Similarly, a SaaS company usually runs two parts: a marketing site that wins sign-ups, and the web app itself. The marketing site needs pricing, trials, security details and a CMS for fast content updates. Meanwhile, the app needs logins, billing and solid back-end systems.

What does a local service website need?

Finally, local businesses win through local SEO. So each location or service area needs its own page with clear details. In addition, online booking and reviews help visitors act quickly. Google explains local ranking in its guide to local results.

How much does each type cost?

Local and B2B sites usually cost the least. Next come ecommerce stores. Finally, SaaS apps and marketplaces cost the most, because they are full software products. Our website development cost guide explains why.

How do you plan for growth?

  1. Choose a platform you will not outgrow soon. Check limits on products, users and integrations.
  2. Keep content portable. Make sure you can export pages and data.
  3. Build in phases. Launch the core first, then add features as revenue grows.

What are common mistakes when choosing a website type?

  • Copying a competitor whose business model is different.
  • Building a marketplace on a simple store platform.
  • Treating a B2B site like a brochure with no lead capture.
  • Underestimating the ongoing cost of a SaaS or marketplace platform.

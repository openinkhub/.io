Before hiring a website development company, check 15 things: portfolio fit, real results, technical stack, security, contract terms, communication, support, SEO skill, code ownership and a realistic timeline, among others. Also ask for references and test their live sites for speed, mobile use and accessibility. This checklist turns those points into simple questions you can ask on a call.

Why use a checklist when hiring a website development company?

When hiring a website development company, every pitch sounds good. So a fixed checklist helps you compare companies on the same points instead of on charm. It also gives you a record of what each team promised, which is useful when you review the contract.

Use the 15 checks below in order. Score each company from one to five on every point, then compare the totals.

Portfolio and experience (checks 1–4)

  1. Relevant portfolio. Do they have live sites in your industry or at your size? Mock-ups alone are not enough.
  2. Measured results. Can they show case studies with before and after numbers, such as leads, sales or load time?
  3. Live-site quality. Open three of their sites on your phone. Are they fast, clear and easy to use?
  4. References. Will they let you speak to two recent clients? Ask those clients about deadlines and support.

For a deeper method, read how to evaluate a website development company portfolio.

Technical skills (checks 5–8)

  1. Technical stack. Which CMS or framework do they suggest, and why? The reason should fit your needs.
  2. Security practice. Do they cover HTTPS, updates, backups, safe logins and form protection?
  3. SEO and speed. Will they set up titles, clean URLs, schema, a sitemap and fast loading? Google explains its speed signals in the Core Web Vitals documentation.
  4. Accessibility. Do they design for keyboard use, colour contrast and screen readers?

Process and communication (checks 9–11)

  1. Named contact. Who manages your project, and will that person stay for the whole build?
  2. Update rhythm. How often will you get progress updates, and in what format?
  3. Realistic timeline. Does the plan include time for content, feedback and testing? A timeline that looks too short usually is.

Contract and pricing (checks 12–15)

  1. Itemised quote. Is every part priced on its own line, including content, SEO and training?
  2. Ownership. Will you own the code, design files, domain and hosting accounts once you pay?
  3. Change terms. How are extra requests priced, and who approves them?
  4. Support after launch. What does the support plan cover, how fast do they respond, and what does it cost each month?

Which questions should you ask a web development company?

  • What did a project like mine cost, and how long did it take?
  • What is not included in this quote?
  • How will you test the site before launch?
  • What happens if you miss the deadline?
  • How do I get my files if we stop working together?

How do you compare pricing fairly?

First, send every company the same brief. Then line up their quotes item by item. If one quote is much lower, find out what is missing. Often it is content, testing, SEO setup or support.

Also compare cost over two years, not just the build. Hosting, updates and small changes add up. Our website development cost guide lists the common line items.

What should the contract include when hiring a website development company?

  • Scope, with pages and features listed.
  • Milestones, deliverables and sign-off points.
  • Payment stages tied to milestones.
  • Ownership of code, content and accounts.
  • Support terms and response times.
  • How either side can end the contract.

What red flags should you watch for?

  • A fixed price before they understand your needs.
  • No live work, or work they cannot explain.
  • They want to register the domain in their own name.
  • No mention of SEO, speed, security or accessibility.
  • Pressure to sign quickly.

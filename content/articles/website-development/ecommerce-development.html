Ecommerce website development in India starts with choosing a platform such as Shopify, WooCommerce or a custom build. Then register your business and GST, set up payments through Razorpay, PayU or Stripe, plan shipping, build product and policy pages, and launch with SEO and analytics. Budgets typically run from ₹50,000 to ₹5 lakh or more, depending on scope.

How to start an ecommerce website in India

  1. Choose your platform. Hosted (Shopify), self-hosted (WooCommerce) or custom.
  2. Handle the paperwork. Register your business and GST before you take payments.
  3. Set up payments. Use a gateway such as Razorpay, PayU or Stripe, with UPI, cards and wallets.
  4. Plan shipping. Choose couriers, set delivery zones and decide on cash on delivery.
  5. Build your catalogue. Clear product photos, descriptions, sizes and prices.
  6. Add trust pages. Shipping, returns, refunds, privacy and contact details.
  7. Launch and market. Set up SEO, analytics, abandoned-cart emails and ads.

Must-have ecommerce website features

  • Fast search and filters, so shoppers find products quickly.
  • Detailed product pages with reviews.
  • A short checkout with guest checkout and several payment options.
  • Shipping and tax calculators.
  • HTTPS and PCI-compliant payments.
  • Abandoned-cart recovery and order updates, for example on WhatsApp.
  • A fast, mobile-first design and good analytics.

The ecommerce website development process

Most stores follow six stages: strategy and platform choice, UX and design, catalogue and content, development and integrations, testing of payments and checkout, and launch with ongoing improvement. A small store can launch in about four weeks. Complex or multi-vendor platforms can take up to six months.

How much does ecommerce website development cost in India?

A small Shopify store can start around ₹50,000. Custom or multi-vendor platforms can cost ₹10 lakh or more. Remember platform fees, payment gateway charges, apps, maintenance and marketing. How to improve ecommerce conversion rates

Start where shoppers drop off. Usually that means a slow site, a long checkout, few payment options or weak product pages. Fix the biggest leak first, then test. Mobile checkout, guest checkout and trust signals usually matter most.

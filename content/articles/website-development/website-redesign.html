A website redesign rebuilds your site's design, structure and often its technology to fix problems updates can't solve. Common signs you need one are a poor mobile experience, slow pages, a dated look, falling conversions, security gaps and content that is hard to update. Plan the redesign carefully so you keep your rankings, URLs and analytics history.

Signs your business website needs a redesign

  • It is hard to use on a phone.
  • Pages load slowly or fail Core Web Vitals.
  • The design no longer matches your brand or looks dated.
  • Visitors arrive but few contact you or buy.
  • Updating content needs a developer every time.
  • It runs on old software that is hard to secure.
  • Your services or business model have changed.

Website redesign vs refresh

A refresh updates the look: colours, fonts, images and some layouts, on the same platform and structure. A redesign rethinks structure, content and often the technology too. Choose a refresh if the site works well but looks tired. Choose a redesign if the problems are in speed, structure, mobile use or how easily you can update it.

The website redesign process

  1. Audit. Review traffic, top pages, rankings, speed and what converts.
  2. Plan. Set goals, map content and decide what to keep, merge or drop.
  3. Map URLs. List every old URL and where it will go.
  4. Design and build. New design, structure and technology.
  5. Test. Check redirects, forms, speed, mobile and tracking before launch.
  6. Launch and monitor. Watch rankings, traffic and errors closely for the first weeks.

How to keep your SEO safe during a redesign

Redesigns lose rankings when URLs change without redirects, pages disappear or the new site is accidentally blocked. To protect your SEO:

  • keep URLs that already rank, wherever possible;
  • add 301 redirects from every old URL that changes;
  • carry over titles, meta descriptions and structured data;
  • remove any “noindex” or staging blocks before launch;
  • submit your new sitemap and watch Search Console.

A lead generation website combines a clear value proposition, focused landing pages, short forms, CRM integration, trust signals, fast loading, mobile-first design and analytics. Then it improves through testing: headlines, offers and form length. Most importantly, track cost per lead and lead quality, not just traffic, so you can prove the site pays for itself.

This guide shows how to build a business website that brings in enquiries, step by step.

How do you build a lead generation website?

  1. Define a qualified lead. Agree with sales what a good enquiry looks like.
  2. Write a clear value proposition. Say who you help, with what, and why you are different.
  3. Create one page per offer. Each service or campaign gets its own focused landing page.
  4. Add lead capture. Use short forms, click-to-call and WhatsApp buttons.
  5. Connect your CRM. Send every lead straight to sales with its source.
  6. Track everything. Set up analytics and conversion goals before launch.
  7. Test and improve. Change one thing at a time and measure the result.

What are landing page best practices?

A landing page has one job: to turn a visitor into a lead. So remove anything that distracts, such as extra menus or unrelated links. Next, match the page headline to the ad or search that brought the visitor. Then place the form or button where it is easy to see on a phone.

In addition, answer the main objections on the page. For example, explain price ranges, timelines and what happens after someone submits the form.

How should lead capture forms be designed?

  • Ask only for what sales truly needs at this stage.
  • Use clear labels above each field, not just placeholder text.
  • Show helpful error messages next to the field.
  • Confirm what happens next on the thank-you page.

Accessible forms convert better for everyone. The W3C explains the basics in its forms tutorial.

Why does CRM integration matter?

Leads lose value quickly. Therefore, every form should send data to your CRM at once, with the page, campaign and keyword that produced it. As a result, sales can follow up fast, and you can see which channels bring real customers.

How do you measure lead generation performance?

  • Conversion rate: the share of visitors who become leads.
  • Cost per lead: marketing spend divided by leads.
  • Lead quality: the share of leads that sales accepts.
  • Revenue per lead: closed sales value divided by leads.

What are common lead generation mistakes?

  • Sending all traffic to the home page instead of focused landing pages.
  • Long forms that ask for budget, company size and more on first contact.
  • No follow-up process, so leads go cold.
  • Measuring traffic only, not leads and sales.

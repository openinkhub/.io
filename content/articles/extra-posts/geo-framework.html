GEO Framework: A 6-Step Plan to Get Cited by AI Answers

A GEO framework is a repeatable plan for generative engine optimization: making your brand easy for AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews to find, trust and cite. Our GEO framework has six steps: audit, entity, answers, evidence, structure and tracking. Most businesses can run the first full cycle in about 30 days.

This guide explains each step, what to check, and how GEO fits with SEO, AEO and AIO.

Why you need a GEO framework

AI tools now answer many questions before anyone clicks a link. So if your brand is not part of those answers, buyers may never see you. A framework helps in three ways:

  • It turns GEO from guesswork into a checklist your team can repeat
  • GEO work then links to the SEO, AEO and AIO work you already do
  • A monthly score shows your progress

Step-by-step: running the GEO framework

Step 1: Audit your AI visibility

First, list the questions buyers ask before they choose a firm like yours. Then ask each one in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. Note whether you are named, which rivals appear and which sources are cited.

Step 2: Fix your entity data

AI tools merge facts from many sites. So your brand name, address, phone and service list must match on your website, Google Business Profile, LinkedIn and directories.

Step 3: Write answer-first content

Use real questions as H2 or H3 headings. Then answer each one in the first 40–60 words, and add detail below.

Step 4: Add evidence and mentions

Publish your own numbers, client results and expert quotes. Also, earn mentions on trusted industry sites, because AI tools lean on sources others already trust.

Step 5: Structure pages for machines

Add Organization, Service and FAQ schema, and test it in Google's Rich Results Test. Keep pages fast, and consider an llms.txt file that lists your key pages. Google's own guide to AI features and your website confirms that normal SEO best practice still applies.

Step 6: Track and repeat

Finally, rerun your question list every month. Track how often you are named and cited, then fix the gaps you find.

How to choose where to start

  • AI tools describe you wrongly: start with Step 2, entity data.
  • Rivals are named and you are not: start with Steps 3 and 4, answers and evidence.
  • You have content but no schema: start with Step 5, structure.
  • You are not sure: start with Step 1 and let the audit decide.

How to build your GEO question list

The audit in Step 1 is only as good as the questions you test. So build the list with care, and reuse it every month.

Where to find real buyer questions

  • Sales calls and WhatsApp chats: note the exact words buyers use before they choose
  • Google Search Console: look for long queries that start with how, what, which or best
  • People Also Ask boxes: search your main topics and copy the related questions
  • Review sites and forums: read what people ask before they hire a firm like yours

Group questions by buying stage

StageExample questionWhat a good AI answer does
Learning"What is GEO and how does it work?"Explains the idea and cites a clear source
Comparing"Which GEO agencies work with SMEs in India?"Names a short list of firms
Deciding"Is Openink Hub good for GEO?"Describes your services and proof accurately

Aim for 20–30 questions across the three stages. In short, that is enough to spot patterns without making the monthly check too slow.

A 30-day plan to run the first GEO cycle

  1. Week 1: audit and entity. Run your question list in ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity. Then fix any brand facts that are wrong or missing on your site, Google Business Profile and LinkedIn.
  2. Week 2: answers. Rewrite your five most important pages so each one opens with a direct, 40–60 word answer under a question heading.
  3. Week 3: evidence and structure. Add one case study or data point to each of those pages. Also add Organization, Service and FAQ schema, then test it.
  4. Week 4: track. Rerun the full question list and compare it with Week 1. Note which answers changed and plan next month's fixes.

Common GEO framework mistakes

  • Testing once and stopping. AI answers change often, so a single check tells you little.
  • Chasing one AI tool only. Buyers use several, so test ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews.
  • Writing for bots, not people. Stuffed, robotic pages lose trust with both readers and AI systems.
  • Ignoring off-site facts. If directories and social profiles disagree with your site, AI tools may repeat the wrong details.
  • Skipping the basics. Slow, hard-to-crawl pages are less likely to be used as sources at all.

Measuring GEO results

Keep a simple monthly sheet. For each question, record whether your brand is named, which page is cited and whether the answer is accurate. Then track three numbers over time:

  1. Mention rate: the share of questions where AI names your brand
  2. Citation rate: how often AI links to one of your pages
  3. Accuracy: whether each answer describes you correctly

As a result, you can show progress to your team, even before AI traffic shows up clearly in analytics.

GEO framework checklist

Use this checklist at the end of each monthly cycle. It keeps the GEO framework on track as your team and content grow.

  • The question list was tested in every AI tool your buyers use
  • Brand name, address and services match across your site and profiles
  • Each key page opens with a short, direct answer
  • Every key page has at least one fact, number or case study
  • Organization, Service and FAQ schema pass the Rich Results Test
  • New mentions on trusted sites were logged
  • The monthly sheet shows mention rate, citation rate and accuracy
  • Next month's fixes are written down, with an owner for each one

Who should own the GEO framework?

GEO touches content, SEO, PR and brand data, so it can easily fall between teams. Give it one owner instead. In a small business, that is often the marketing lead or the founder. In a larger team, the SEO lead usually owns it, with help from content and PR.

The owner does not have to do every task. However, they should run the monthly audit, keep the sheet up to date and make sure each fix has a name and a date next to it. Also, share a one-page summary with the wider team each month, so everyone sees how AI tools now describe the brand.

Frequently asked questions

What is a GEO framework?

It is a step-by-step plan for generative engine optimization. It helps AI tools find, trust and cite your brand in their answers.

How long does the GEO framework take?

The first full cycle usually takes about 30 days. After that, a monthly check keeps it up to date.

Do I need SEO before GEO?

Yes, ideally. AI tools often draw on pages that already rank and load well, so SEO gives GEO a strong base.

How do I measure GEO results?

Ask the same buyer questions in AI tools each month. Then track how often your brand is named, which pages are cited and how accurate the answers are.

Which AI tools should a GEO framework cover?

Cover the tools your buyers use most. For most firms, that means ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews.

Can a small business run a GEO framework without an agency?

Yes. The audit and tracking steps need only time and a sheet. However, schema, content rewrites and earning mentions are faster with expert help.

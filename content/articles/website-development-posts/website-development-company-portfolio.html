To evaluate a website development company portfolio, check how relevant the work is to your industry, what measurable results it achieved, how good the live sites are, how complex the projects were, and what past clients say. Ask for case studies with before and after numbers. Then test the sites yourself on mobile, for speed and for accessibility.

This guide gives you a simple method you can follow without technical knowledge.

Why the website development company portfolio matters most

A sales pitch tells you what a company says it can do. By contrast, a website development company portfolio shows what it has actually done. So the portfolio is the best evidence you have before you sign.

However, a portfolio is also a sales tool. Companies pick their best screenshots. That is why you need to look past the images and test the real sites.

How do you evaluate a portfolio in five steps?

  1. Check relevance. Look for work in your sector or with a similar goal. For example, if you sell online, you need ecommerce examples, not only brochure sites.
  2. Open the live sites. Visit at least three of them. If a site is offline or very different from the screenshot, ask why.
  3. Test on your phone. Most visitors arrive on mobile. Check that menus work, text is readable and forms are easy to fill in.
  4. Check the speed. Run each page through Google PageSpeed Insights. Google explains the scores in its Core Web Vitals documentation.
  5. Look at complexity. Note which sites have logins, payments, search, bookings or integrations. Those show deeper skill than simple pages.

Which case studies and metrics should you look for?

A strong case study tells a short story: the problem, what the team did, and what changed. In particular, look for numbers the client cares about.

MetricWhat it tells you
Leads or enquiriesShows whether the new site brought in more business
Conversion rateShows if more visitors took action
Organic trafficReveals whether search visibility improved after launch
Page load timeProves the site got faster
Time to launchTells you if they delivered on schedule

If a case study has no numbers, ask for them. A company that measured nothing may not know if its work helped.

How do you check references and reviews?

Ask for two or three recent clients you can contact. Then ask them simple questions:

  • Did the project finish on time and on budget?
  • How did the team handle changes and problems?
  • Are you still working with them after launch?
  • Would you hire them again?

Also read reviews on independent sites, such as Google Business Profile listings. Look at how the company replies to criticism, not just at the star rating.

How do you judge web development company experience?

Years in business help, but they are not the whole picture. Instead, ask how many projects like yours the team has finished recently. Ask who on the current team worked on them. In addition, check that they still support those sites today.

Experience also shows in the questions they ask you. A seasoned team asks about goals, audience, content and growth before it talks about colours.

What red flags should you watch for?

  • Only mock-ups or templates, with no live sites.
  • Live sites that are slow, broken or hard to use on a phone.
  • Case studies without any measured result.
  • No clients willing to give a reference.
  • Every site looks the same, which suggests one template for all.

Does experience affect pricing?

Usually, yes. Teams with deep experience often charge more per day. Even so, they may finish faster and make fewer mistakes, so the total cost can be similar or lower.
